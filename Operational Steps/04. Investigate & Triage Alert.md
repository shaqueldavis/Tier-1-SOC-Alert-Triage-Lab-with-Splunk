#  Investigate & Triage Alert

### Process

As a SOC Analyst we want to follow a repeatable process while investigating this alert:
```text
Alert triggered
     ↓
Validate the alert
     ↓
Establish scope
     ↓
Identify account + source
     ↓
Determine logon type
     ↓
Look for related activity
     ↓
Assess malicious vs. benign
     ↓
Determine severity/disposition
     ↓
Document findings
     ↓
Escalate or close
```

Our goal is to answer What happened, who/what was involved, how significant is it, and what should happen next?

This is where we shift our mind. Naturally, we know everything that just happened because we engineered the detection. However, we are switching hats and approaching this from the perspective that we are now a SOC analyst at work. One who has not seen this alert before. From that point of view, we now hold the responsibility of Investigating the legitimacy and severity of an unknown potential threat. 


### Start With the Alert

There are a few questions that we want answered immediately:
- What was detected?
- When was it detected?
- what is the severity?
- what is the source?

Some of this information can be found by simply looking at the alert, before doing any additional investigation.

The name of the alert alone tells us what was detected, "Repeat Failed Login Attempts". That's our first answer

On the Triggered Alerts page within our Splunk dashboard a few things are already provided for us.
- Alert name: `Repeat Failed Login Attempts`
- Time: `2026-08-26 15:05:00 UTC` or 3:05 ET as it relates to me.
- Severity: `Medium` 
That answers 3 of our first 4 questions



The beginning of the information we need for our investigation can be found directly in the alert itself. From the Triggered Alerts page on the Splunk dashboard, click View Results next to the alert.
